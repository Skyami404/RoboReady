# VEX IQ Competition Rules Summary

## VEX IQ Overview

VEX IQ is designed for elementary and middle school students (ages 8-14). It features a simplified building system and focuses on fundamental robotics concepts.

## Robot Construction Rules

**Size Constraints:**
- Maximum starting size: 11" x 19" x 15" (LxWxH)
- Can expand during match
- Must fit within size constraint at start

**Legal Parts:**
- Only official VEX IQ parts
- Custom 3D printed parts allowed (with restrictions)
- Non-VEX electronics prohibited
- Decorations allowed if safe

**Motors:**
- Maximum 4 motors per robot
- Must be VEX IQ Smart Motors
- Connected to IQ Brain

**Brain and Controller:**
- One VEX IQ Brain (1st gen or 2nd gen)
- One VEX IQ Controller
- Battery must be secured

## Match Format

**Match Length:**
- Total: 60 seconds
- Autonomous: 15 seconds (driver cannot control)
- Driver Control: 45 seconds (manual control)

**Match Types:**
- Teamwork matches (2v2 alliance)
- Skills matches (solo, autonomous + driver)

**Field:**
- 6 feet x 8 feet
- Foam tiles
- Field elements vary by game

## Scoring and Ranking

**Qualification Matches:**
- Win Points (WP): 2 for win, 1 for tie, 0 for loss
- Autonomous Win Points (AWP): 1 if win autonomous period
- Strength of Schedule (SOS): Tiebreaker based on opponents

**Rankings:**
1. Win Points (highest)
2. Autonomous Win Points (highest)
3. Strength of Schedule (highest)
4. Total Points scored (highest)

## Alliance Selection

- Top 4-8 teams (varies by tournament size)
- Alliance captains select partners
- Selected teams can accept or decline
- Elimination bracket follows

## Skills Challenge

**Two Components:**
1. **Driving Skills:** 60 seconds manual control
2. **Autonomous Coding Skills:** 60 seconds fully autonomous

**Skills Ranking:**
- Combined score from both challenges
- Can attempt multiple times per tournament
- Best score counts
- Separate global rankings

## Safety and Conduct

**Safety Requirements:**
- No sharp edges or dangerous mechanisms
- Secure all parts and fasteners
- Battery properly mounted
- Emergency stops accessible

**Student-Centered:**
- Students must do majority of work
- Adults can mentor but not build
- Students drive, program, and present

**Gracious Professionalism:**
- Respect opponents and officials
- Help other teams
- Compete with integrity
- Learn from wins and losses

## Tournament Structure

1. **Robot Inspection:** Verify size, safety, legality
2. **Practice Matches:** (optional) Test robot on field
3. **Qualification Matches:** 4-6 matches per team
4. **Skills Challenges:** Attempt throughout day
5. **Alliance Selection:** Top teams choose partners
6. **Elimination Matches:** Single elimination bracket
7. **Awards Ceremony:** Recognize achievements

## Awards

**Excellence Award:**
- Overall best team
- Robot performance + notebook + interview
- Advances to higher-level competitions

**Other Major Awards:**
- Robot Skills Champion
- Tournament Champions (1st place)
- Tournament Finalists (2nd place)
- Design Award
- Judges Award
- Innovate Award
- Think Award (engineering notebook)

## Engineering Notebook

**Required for Excellence Award:**
- Document design process
- Include sketches, photos, reflections
- Show iterations and improvements
- Demonstrate problem-solving
- Authentic work by students

**Best Practices:**
- Regular entries (date each one)
- Write in past tense
- Include failures and successes
- Be specific and detailed
- Make it your own (not templated)

## Game-Specific Rules

Each season has a unique game with specific rules for:
- Scoring objects and methods
- Field elements and interactions
- Starting positions
- Restricted actions
- Autonomous bonuses

**Always refer to current season's game manual!**

## Driver and Programming

**Drivers:**
- Two students per alliance (one per robot)
- Cannot touch field or robots during match
- Must stay in designated area
- Can communicate with partner

**Programming:**
- Must be student work
- Pre-loaded before match
- Cannot be modified during match
- Autonomous must be fully automatic

## Common Violations

**Major Penalties (Disqualification):**
- Dangerous robot behavior
- Unsportsmanlike conduct
- Intentional field/robot damage
- Adult intervention during match

**Minor Penalties (Point deductions):**
- Exceeding size constraints
- Improper field interaction
- Rule violations during match

## IQ vs V5 Differences

| Aspect | VEX IQ | VEX V5 |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Age Group | 8-14 years | 11-18+ years |
| Field Size | 6'×8' | 12'×12' |
| Build System | Plastic | Metal |
| Motors | Max 4 | Max 8 |
| Match Length | 60 seconds | 2 minutes |
| Complexity | Beginner-Intermediate | Intermediate-Advanced |

## Tips for Success

1. **Start Simple:** Master basics before adding complexity
2. **Test Often:** Regular testing prevents surprises
3. **Document Everything:** Engineering notebook is crucial
4. **Practice Driving:** Skills matter as much as robot design
5. **Learn from Others:** Watch matches, ask questions
6. **Have Fun:** Enjoy the learning process!

Remember: VEX IQ is about learning fundamentals, teamwork, and problem-solving. Focus on growth and improvement, not just winning!
