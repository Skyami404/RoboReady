# VEX Robotics Building Guide

## Robot Design Fundamentals

### Drivetrain Options

**Tank Drive**
- Most common and reliable
- Two sides of wheels controlled independently
- Easy to program and maintain
- Good pushing power
- Best for: Beginners, pushing games

**X-Drive (Holonomic)**
- Four wheels at 45-degree angles
- Can strafe (move sideways)
- Very maneuverable
- More complex programming
- Best for: Experienced teams, positioning games

**H-Drive**
- Tank drive plus center strafe wheel
- Good balance of power and maneuverability
- Can push and strafe
- Best for: Medium complexity games

**Mecanum Drive**
- Special rollers on wheels
- Full omni-directional movement
- Requires precise alignment
- Less pushing power
- Best for: Precision positioning

### Key Design Principles

**1. Center of Gravity**
- Keep weight low and centered
- Prevents tipping
- Improves stability
- Better control

**2. Gear Ratios**
- Speed vs Torque tradeoff
- High speed: Good for racing, moving quickly
- High torque: Good for pushing, lifting
- Common ratios: 1:1 (balanced), 3:5 (speed), 5:3 (torque)

**3. Structural Integrity**
- Use proper bracing (triangles are strongest)
- Avoid long unsupported spans
- Secure all connections
- Check for wobble

**4. Weight Distribution**
- Balance front and back
- Don't make robot too heavy
- Use lightweight materials where possible
- Strategic weight placement for traction

**5. Modularity**
- Build in sections (drivetrain, intake, lift, etc.)
- Easy to modify and repair
- Test subsystems independently
- Allows parallel development

### Common Mechanisms

**Intakes**
- Roller intake: Spinning wheels pull objects in
- Claw: Grabs and holds objects
- Conveyor: Belt system moves objects
- Considerations: Speed, consistency, object compatibility

**Lifts**
- Four-bar linkage: Smooth arc motion
- Linear lift: Straight up and down
- Scissor lift: Compact, extends vertically
- Considerations: Height, speed, stability

**Launchers**
- Flywheel: Spinning wheel launches objects
- Catapult: Stored energy release
- Puncher: Linear impact
- Considerations: Consistency, power, reload time

### Building Best Practices

**Spacing**
- Use proper spacers between structural pieces
- Prevents friction and binding
- Allows smooth rotation
- Standard spacing: 0.5", 1", 2"

**Fasteners**
- Use lock nuts on critical joints
- Don't overtighten (strips threads)
- Use nylock nuts to prevent loosening
- Threadlocker for vibration-prone areas

**Motor Placement**
- Mount securely with proper support
- Consider heat dissipation
- Easy access for troubleshooting
- Protect from impacts

**Wire Management**
- Keep wires organized and secured
- Use zip ties or wire clips
- Avoid pinch points and moving parts
- Leave slack for movement

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Overbuilding**: Too complex, hard to maintain
2. **Underbracing**: Weak structure, breaks easily
3. **Poor wire management**: Wires get caught, disconnected
4. **Ignoring friction**: Binding, inconsistent performance
5. **Not testing early**: Problems discovered too late
6. **Copying without understanding**: Can't troubleshoot
7. **Neglecting driver practice**: Great robot, poor performance

### Robot Inspection Checklist

**Size Constraints**
- Starting size: 18" x 18" x 18" (check game manual)
- Measure with robot in starting configuration
- Account for tolerances

**Safety**
- No sharp edges or points
- All screws properly installed
- No exposed wiring
- Battery secure

**Rules Compliance**
- Legal parts only
- Proper motor/sensor usage
- Team identification visible
- Follows game-specific rules

### Tips for Success

**Iteration**
- Build, test, improve, repeat
- Don't expect perfection on first try
- Document what works and what doesn't
- Learn from failures

**Simplicity**
- Simple designs are more reliable
- Easier to troubleshoot and repair
- Focus on doing a few things well
- Add complexity only if needed

**Practice**
- Build multiple prototypes
- Test in realistic conditions
- Practice with drivers
- Time your operations

**Documentation**
- Take photos during build process
- Document design decisions
- Keep track of gear ratios and specs
- Update engineering notebook
