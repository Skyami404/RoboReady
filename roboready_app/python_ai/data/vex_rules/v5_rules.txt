# VEX V5 Competition Rules Summary

## V5 System Overview

VEX V5 is designed for middle school, high school, and college students. It features a metal building system and more advanced electronics compared to VEX IQ.

## Robot Construction Rules

**Size Constraints:**
- Maximum starting size: 18" x 18" x 18" (cube)
- Can expand during match
- Must fit in size constraint at match start
- Measured with all mechanisms in starting position

**Legal Parts:**
- Only official VEX V5 and VEX EDR parts
- Some non-VEX parts allowed (raw materials, fasteners)
- Custom 3D printed parts allowed (with restrictions)
- Non-VEX electronics prohibited except approved sensors

**Motors:**
- Maximum 8 motors (11W or 5.5W V5 Smart Motors)
- Maximum 4 motors per motor group without splitters
- Must connect to V5 Brain smart ports

**Pneumatics:**
- Allowed with restrictions
- Maximum 100 PSI
- Proper safety equipment required
- Reservoirs must be VEX legal

**Brain, Controller, Battery:**
- One V5 Brain or V5 Brain Pro
- One V5 Controller
- One V5 Battery (secured properly)
- Wireless communication via VEXnet 3.0

## Match Format

**Match Length:**
- Total: 2 minutes (120 seconds)
- Autonomous: 15 seconds (no driver input)
- Driver Control: 1 minute 45 seconds (manual control)

**Match Types:**
- Teamwork Challenge: 2v2 alliances
- Skills Challenge: Solo robot (driver skills + programming skills)

**Field:**
- 12 feet x 12 feet
- Foam tiles with perimeter
- Game-specific elements

## Scoring and Ranking

**Qualification Matches:**
- **Win Points (WP):** 2 for win, 1 for tie, 0 for loss
- **Autonomous Win Points (AWP):** 1 if alliance wins autonomous period
- **Strength of Schedule (SOS):** Sum of opponent and alliance partner WP
- **Total Points (SP):** Combined match score

**Ranking Order:**
1. Win Points (WP) - highest
2. Autonomous Win Points (AWP) - highest
3. Strength of Schedule (SOS) - highest
4. Total Points (SP) - highest
5. Autonomous Points - highest

## Alliance Selection

**Process:**
- Top 8-16 teams become alliance captains (varies by tournament)
- Captains ranked by qualification performance
- Selection serpentine order: 1,2,3...,n,n,...,3,2,1
- Teams can accept or decline invitations
- If declined, captain selects again
- Continues until all alliances complete

**Strategy:**
- Choose complementary robots
- Consider reliability and consistency
- Communication and teamwork matter
- Have backup choices prepared

## Elimination Matches

**Format:**
- Best-of-3 series
- Alliance must win 2 matches to advance
- Single elimination bracket
- Quarterfinals → Semifinals → Finals

**Advantages:**
- Higher seed chooses starting position
- In case of 1-1 tie in series, higher seed wins

## Skills Challenge

**Two Components:**

**1. Driver Skills (60 seconds):**
- Manual control only
- Robot alone on field
- Maximize score
- Can attempt multiple times per tournament

**2. Programming Skills (60 seconds):**
- Fully autonomous
- No driver input
- Pre-programmed routine
- Can attempt multiple times per tournament

**Combined Ranking:**
- Highest driver skills + highest programming skills
- Separate global rankings
- Can qualify for championships via skills

## Safety and Construction

**Safety Requirements:**
- No sharp edges or points
- No exposed electrical connections
- All screws properly installed
- Battery secured with legal mounting
- Emergency stop accessible

**Prohibited Items:**
- Hydraulics
- Combustion engines
- Hazardous materials
- Anything that damages field
- Entanglement devices (intentional)

**Inspection Checklist:**
- Size within limits
- Legal parts only
- Safe construction
- Team identification visible
- Passes all game-specific rules

## Student-Centered Policy

**Students Must:**
- Design and build majority of robot
- Write all code
- Drive during matches
- Explain design to judges

**Adults Can:**
- Teach skills and concepts
- Advise on strategy
- Ensure safety
- Provide resources

**Not Allowed:**
- Adults building robot for students
- Pre-built robots from kits (beyond basics)
- Purchased code or designs
- Adults doing student work

## Conduct and Sportsmanship

**Expected Behavior:**
- Respect opponents, refs, volunteers
- Gracious professionalism
- Help other teams when possible
- Accept decisions gracefully
- Learn from every experience

**Violations:**
- Unsportsmanlike conduct can result in:
  - Warnings
  - Match disqualifications
  - Tournament disqualifications
  - Ineligibility for awards

## Tournament Structure

**Day of Competition:**
1. Check-in and pit setup
2. Robot inspection
3. Practice matches (if available)
4. Qualification matches (typically 5-8 per team)
5. Skills challenge attempts (throughout day)
6. Alliance selection
7. Elimination rounds
8. Awards ceremony

**Typical Duration:** 6-10 hours

## Awards

**Major Awards:**
- **Excellence Award:** Best overall (robot + notebook + interview)
- **Tournament Champions:** Winners of eliminations
- **Design Award:** Outstanding engineering design and process
- **Judges Award:** Exemplifies VEX program values
- **Innovate Award:** Creative and unique design solution
- **Build Award:** High-quality construction
- **Think Award:** Best engineering notebook
- **Amaze Award:** Best driver skills performance
- **Skills Challenge Champion:** Highest combined skills score

**Award Advancement:**
- Excellence: Typically advances to next level
- Champions: Often advances to next level
- Varies by region and event level

## Engineering Notebook

**Purpose:**
- Document design process
- Show problem-solving
- Demonstrate learning
- Required for design-related awards

**Contents:**
- Team information
- Meeting notes and dates
- Design iterations
- Testing results
- Code documentation
- Photos and sketches
- Reflections and improvements
- Competition records

**Best Practices:**
- Regular, dated entries
- Written by students
- Authentic (not templates)
- Detailed and specific
- Shows evolution of design

## Game-Specific Rules

**Each Season Features:**
- Unique game with specific objectives
- Specific scoring methods
- Field elements and layout
- Starting configurations
- Restricted actions
- Autonomous bonuses

**Current Season:**
- Always refer to official game manual
- Updated Q&A rulings on RobotEvents
- Addendums and clarifications released

## Common Rules Across Games

**General Restrictions:**
- No intentional field damage
- No detached parts
- No entanglement
- No interference with opponents' controls
- Stay in designated zones

**Scoring:**
- Points counted at end of match
- Referees have final say
- Video review available (for finals typically)
- Gracious professionalism in disputes

## Violations and Penalties

**Minor Violations:**
- Point deductions
- Warnings
- Specific penalties per game manual

**Major Violations:**
- Match disqualification
- Safety issues
- Egregious rule breaking
- Dangerous behavior

**Repeated Violations:**
- Can result in tournament disqualification
- Affect award eligibility
- Noted in team record

## Programming Requirements

**Autonomous:**
- Must be fully automatic
- No driver input allowed
- Pre-loaded before match
- Cannot be modified during match

**Driver Control:**
- Manual control via controller
- Can have automated functions
- Driver controls overall robot behavior

**Code Management:**
- Must be student work
- Document in engineering notebook
- Available for review if requested

## V5 vs IQ Differences

| Aspect | V5 | IQ |
|--------|----|----|
| Age Group | 11-18+ years | 8-14 years |
| Field | 12'×12' | 6'×8' |
| Building | Metal | Plastic |
| Motors | Max 8 | Max 4 |
| Match | 2 minutes | 60 seconds |
| Complexity | Advanced | Beginner |

## Tips for V5 Success

1. **Master the Manual:** Read thoroughly, multiple times
2. **Plan Autonomous:** Crucial for rankings
3. **Reliable > Complex:** Consistency wins
4. **Practice Driving:** Skilled drivers make big difference
5. **Document Everything:** Notebook matters for awards
6. **Test Early, Test Often:** Catch problems before tournament
7. **Network:** Learn from other teams
8. **Have Fun:** Remember why you love robotics!

## Key Differences from IQ

- More complex building (metal vs plastic)
- Longer matches (2 min vs 1 min)
- More motors and power
- Higher level of competition
- More sophisticated strategies
- College division available (VEX U)

Remember: Rules are designed for fair, safe competition. When in doubt, ask a referee or check the official Q&A. Focus on learning, improving, and having a great time!
